1D watershed tie-break example
==============================

Grid (distance values)          Seeds
Index :  0   1   2   3   4       0 = seed A        4 = seed B
dgrid :  3   1   1   1   3       (peak)            (peak)
        ^       ^       ^
      peak    saddle    peak

The saddle (indices 1-3) is perfectly flat — every voxel has the same
priority.  The two algorithms therefore resolve every saddle voxel by
tie-break, not by value.

---

A.  skimage  (min-heap, age tie-break)
    Priority = -dgrid, then earliest-push wins.

    Step 1  push A(age=0) and B(age=1)          heap: [A0 , B1]
    Step 2  pop A (age wins tie).  Flood 1 -> A  heap: [B1 , A2]
    Step 3  pop B (value=-3 beats -1). Flood 3 -> B  heap: [A2 , B3]
    Step 4  pop A (age 2 < 3).     Flood 2 -> A  heap: [B3]
    Step 5  pop B.  Neighbour 2 already A. Done.

    Labels:  A   A   A   B   B
             ^^^^^^^^^^^^
             whole saddle goes to A

    skimage gives the ENTIRE flat saddle to whichever seed happened to
    reach the first saddle voxel one step earlier.  On a 1-voxel saddle
    the other seed never even touches it.

---

B.  Rust heap_watershed_impl  (max-heap, flat_index tie-break)
    Priority = +dgrid, then highest flat index wins.

    Step 1  push A(3,idx=0) and B(3,idx=4)       heap: [B(4) , A(0)]
    Step 2  pop B (idx 4 wins tie).  Flood 3 -> B heap: [A(0) , B(1)]
    Step 3  pop A (value 3 beats 1). Flood 1 -> A heap: [B(3) , A(1)]
    Step 4  pop B (idx 3 > 1).       Flood 2 -> B heap: [A(1)]
    Step 5  pop A.  Neighbour 2 already B. Done.

    Labels:  A   A   B   B   B
             ^^^^^^
             saddle split roughly in half

    Rust splits the saddle arbitrarily by memory address (high index
    favoured).  The split has no geometric meaning, but it does mean
    both seeds get a foothold on the saddle.

---

What changes if the saddle is only ONE voxel wide?

Grid:    3   1   3
Index:   0   1   2
Seed A at 0,  Seed B at 2

skimage  ->  A   A   B        (A monopolises the bridge)
Rust     ->  A   B   B        (B monopolises the bridge)

Both are greedy.  The difference is WHICH seed gets the monopoly.
skimage always favours the seed that is graph-distance-closest; on a
flat 1-voxel bridge that is whichever happens to be 1 step nearer in
the ravelled ordering.

---

Why your 0.3-Å window disappears in skimage
--------------------------------------------
A narrow pore window is exactly this 1-voxel (or 2-voxel) flat saddle.
Because skimage's age-based flood gives the WHOLE bridge to whichever
side arrives first, the two regions never share a boundary voxel and
the adjacency graph reports "no connection".  The window is physically
there (voxels exist), but the watershed label field has swallowed it.

Rust, by blindly splitting on flat-index, accidentally leaves one or
two boundary voxels for the other side, so the regions touch and the
adjacency graph keeps the edge.

Neither is truly "correct" for topology.  Both are artefacts of greedy
flood-fill on a quantized grid.
